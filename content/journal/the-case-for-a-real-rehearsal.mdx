Clients ask us to skip the rehearsal more than any other line item — it feels
optional when the guest list is small, or the wedding party is close friends who
"already know what to do." We push back every time.

## What a rehearsal is actually for

It's not really about learning where to stand. It's the one chance to catch a
timing problem, a sightline nobody thought about, or a cue that depends on someone
who isn't actually free at that moment — before it's happening in front of two
hundred guests.

## The twenty-guest wedding still needs one

Smaller events get less margin for error, not more. There's no back-of-house crew
to quietly fix a mistake out of frame. We run a compressed version — twenty
minutes, not two hours — but we run it.

> A real tradition, not staged for content.

## One rule we don't bend

Whoever is walking someone down an aisle, lighting a candle, or giving a toast
does it once, out loud, before the actual moment. Every time we've skipped this,
we've regretted it. Every time we haven't, nobody but us ever knew there was
anything to worry about.
